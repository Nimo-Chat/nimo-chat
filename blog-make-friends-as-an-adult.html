How to Make Friends as an Adult (When Everyone Seems Too Busy)

Published on: November 17, 2026

In school and college, friendship happened almost by itself. You saw the same people every day and shared breaks, group projects and the lunch line. Then come jobs, moving to a new city, friends who get married, have kids or simply disappear into their routines. And one day you realize you haven't met anyone new in months.

If that sounds familiar, nothing is wrong with you. Making friends after 25 is harder for almost everyone, and understanding why helps a lot.

Why it gets harder

Sociologists who study friendship usually point to three conditions for it to form:

  1. Proximity: being near the same people.
  2. Repeated, unplanned interactions: running into each other again and again without having to schedule it.
  3. A setting where people can let their guard down and open up a little.

School gives you all three for free. Adult life rarely does. Work gives you proximity, but you can't always be yourself there. The gym gives you repetition, but nobody talks with headphones on.

Then there's time. A 2018 study by Jeffrey Hall at the University of Kansas estimated that it takes about 50 hours together to go from acquaintance to casual friend, around 90 hours to become a friend, and more than 200 hours to become a close friend. Nobody adds up 90 hours by accident when their calendar is already full.

The good news: you can recreate those conditions on purpose.

7 steps that work

1. Choose repetition over events

One big event does little. A weekly activity does a lot more: a dance class, a running group, volunteering, a pickup game, a book club. The secret is seeing the same people many times until conversation comes naturally.

2. Follow your interests, not "finding friends"

It's easier to start talking when there's already a shared topic. Instead of looking for "people to be friends with," look for people who like what you like. The friendship comes as a side effect.

3. Be the one who reaches out

Almost everyone is waiting for someone else to make the first move. Whoever invites first, for a coffee or to pick up a conversation the next day, is almost always welcome. The worst case is "this week doesn't work," and that's fine.

4. Say yes more often

As an adult it's easy to fall into "I'll pass this time." Every invitation you turn down is an hour of time together you lose. You don't have to say yes to everything, just a bit more often than you do now.

5. Use the internet as a way in

Chatting online doesn't replace in-person friendship, but it's a great start, especially if you're on the quiet side or new in town. In text, you get to know someone by what they think before anything else. If you want a starting point, we wrote a guide on how to make friends online when you're shy.

6. Follow up

A good conversation is only step one. What turns an acquaintance into a friend is coming back: "hey, how did that interview go?" Small, frequent check-ins are worth more than one huge conversation followed by silence.

7. Be patient with yourself

Some attempts won't go anywhere, and that says nothing about you. Adult friendship is a game of volume and time. People who keep trying almost always find their people.
