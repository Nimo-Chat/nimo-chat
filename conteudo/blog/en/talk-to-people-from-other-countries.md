# How to Talk to People From Other Countries (and Practice a Language) on Your Phone

There's one way to learn English, Spanish or any other language that no course app can replace: talking to people who actually speak it. With slang, typos and jokes you don't get at first. That's where a language stops being a school subject and becomes something alive.

And you don't even have to be studying. Chatting with someone on the other side of the world is one of the best things the internet offers: you find out what daily life is like in Tokyo, what people eat for breakfast in Turkey, what someone in Brazil thinks about where you live.

This guide is about doing that well, and safely.

## Why text is the best place to start

Jumping straight into a voice call with a stranger in another language scares a lot of people. Text takes that pressure off:

- **You have time to think.** You can build the sentence calmly, with no one waiting.
- **You can look things up.** A word you don't know can be checked mid-conversation.
- **Mistakes feel lighter.** A typo in a chat is much less awkward than freezing mid-sentence.
- **You learn how people really write.** Abbreviations, slang and expressions no textbook teaches.

After a while, voice messages are the natural next step: you train your ear without having to reply on the spot.

## 6 tips to keep the conversation going

### 1. Say where you're from and what you're learning

"I'm from Canada and I'm practicing my Spanish, feel free to correct me!" is a great first message. Most people love to help, and you've already got a topic.

### 2. Ask about daily life, not clichés

Instead of "what's your country like?", try "what did you have for lunch?" or "what's a normal Saturday like there?". Concrete questions get answers you'd never read in a travel guide. If you need ideas, here are [45 icebreaker questions](blog-icebreaker-questions-for-strangers.html).

### 3. Offer a trade

If they want to learn your language, teach them too. Half the conversation in one language and half in the other is a fair deal, and it makes both of you comfortable making mistakes.

### 4. Mind the time zone

Your "good night" may be someone else's "good morning." Don't be surprised if a reply comes hours later.

### 5. Respect the differences

Humor, religion, politics and even greetings vary a lot from country to country. When in doubt, ask instead of assuming.

### 6. Protect your information

The usual rules for any online chat apply: no address, ID or bank details, and be wary of anyone who asks for money or tries to move the conversation off the app too quickly. Our [safety tips](blog-safety-tips-anonymous-chat.html) cover the warning signs.

## Translation and transcription on Nimo

**Nimo Chat** is available in 14 languages, and two tools help a lot when the other person writes or speaks in a language you haven't mastered yet:

- **Translate message:** when someone uses the app in another language, **Translate** shows up under their message. One tap and you read it in your language. It works in private chats and in group discussions.
- **Transcribe audio:** under every voice message there's **Transcribe**. The audio becomes text, which helps a lot with a tricky accent or a sentence that went by too fast.
- **Playback speed:** you can listen at 1.5x or 2x and skip to any point in the message. To train your ear, try it the other way around: listen at normal speed, read the transcription, then listen again.

The free plan includes 30 translations and 10 transcriptions a day; Premium has no limit. Before the first use, the app asks for your permission and explains that the text or audio of that message is sent to an AI service only to produce the translation or transcription. Nothing is sent unless you tap the button.

A tip for learners: try to understand on your own first and use translation only to check. That way the tool becomes a teacher, not a crutch.

Talking to people from other countries is one of the most fun ways to learn a language and to see the world through someone else's eyes. All it takes is the first message.
