# Group Chat With Strangers: How Discussions Work on Nimo

One-on-one chats are great, but some topics are better in a group. A movie that just came out, a career question, that classic debate like "does pineapple belong on pizza?". With more people, a conversation gets different opinions, more laughs and far fewer awkward silences.

That's why Nimo created **Group discussions**: a way to turn a topic on the Wall into a chat with several people at once, without having to know anyone beforehand.

## What a discussion is

A discussion is a group chat that starts from a note on the Wall. You write a topic, choose **Group discussion** and post it. Anyone interested taps **Join the chat** and lands right in the group with everyone already there.

There are only a few rules, and they're there to keep the conversation good:

- **Up to 15 people.** Enough for variety, few enough that everyone gets to talk.
- **Ends on its own after 24 hours.** A discussion has a beginning, a middle and an end. No abandoned groups with 300 unanswered messages.
- **Text only.** No photos or audio, which keeps the focus on the topic and the space safer.
- **The person who started it can remove anyone who crosses the line.** And anyone can report or block someone with one tap.

Messages go through the same automatic text filter as the Wall.

## How to start your own

1. Open the **Wall** and tap to write.
2. Choose **Group discussion** instead of a note.
3. Write the topic. The more specific, the better: "What show did you binge and regret?" works much better than "let's talk?".
4. Tap **Post discussion**.

That's it. Whoever replies joins the chat with you. Until someone shows up, your discussion stays on the Wall for anyone passing by. If you want to bring people in from outside, you can share the discussion link to Stories or WhatsApp.

## The discussion of the day

Every day at 3 p.m. Brasília time (6 p.m. UTC), Nimo's official account opens a **Discussion of the day** in Portuguese, Spanish and English. It's the easiest way to start: the topic is ready and people keep arriving. If it fills up, the app suggests topics so you can open your own.

## From group to friendship

The best part of a discussion is that it's a showcase of people you already have something in common with. If someone in the group caught your attention, tap their nickname to:

- **chat privately**;
- **send a friend request**;
- **report** or **block** them if needed.

And if someone writes in another language, the **Translate** button works in discussions too.

## Topic ideas that usually work

- "What's the best advice you've ever received?"
- "Recommend me a song nobody knows."
- "What hobby do you want to start but never had the courage to?"
- "Most overrated food in your country. Go."
- "What would you do with a whole free day, no phone?"
- "What job would you have if you could start over?"

Open topics that ask for an opinion or a personal story always work better than yes-or-no questions. For more inspiration, check out our [45 icebreaker questions](blog-icebreaker-questions-for-strangers.html).

## Group etiquette

- **Say hi when you join.** A quick "hey everyone!" breaks the ice.
- **Reply to people, not just the topic.** A chat stays alive when people talk to each other.
- **Disagree without attacking.** Different opinions are what make a discussion interesting.
- **Don't push for private chats.** If someone doesn't want to talk one-on-one, respect it.

Discussions combine the best of the Wall with the best of a good group conversation: new people, a good topic and a time to wrap up. If you haven't tried the Wall yet, start with [how the Wall helps you meet new people](blog-the-board-meet-new-people.html).
