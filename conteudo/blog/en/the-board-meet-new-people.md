# The Board: how to meet new people even when nobody's online

Every random chat app hides the same problem: what happens when there's nobody available to match with? Most of them just leave you staring at an empty screen with a search button and a "try again later" — the same old dead end. Nimo handles this differently, with the Board.

## What the Board is

The Board lives inside the Discover tab, and it's public — quite different from the Feed, which is closed and friends-only. The idea is simple: instead of staring at an empty screen waiting for someone to show up, you leave an "opener" — a question, a topic, a short hook to start a conversation — and anyone passing through can reply to it. It's the app's answer to that moment when live matching can't find anyone, but you still want to talk to someone new.

## How it works in practice

You write what you want to ask or say, with an optional topic for context, and post it. From there, other people browsing the Board can reply to your opener, and you can see how many replies have come in. Each post stays up for 24 hours — after that, it's gone. It's not a permanent wall of accumulated posts: it's meant to be a living stream, always with relatively fresh content, instead of turning into an endless archive of old messages nobody will answer anymore.

## Why this matters

The difference between a random chat app people abandon after one day and one they keep coming back to usually comes down to exactly this: what happens when the luck of matching doesn't work out. An app that leaves you stuck on a dead screen teaches people to give up. An app that offers an alternative path — even a slower one, even one that depends on other people replying — teaches people to come back later. The Board is that alternative path: you're never left empty-handed just because it's a slow hour.

And like everything else in Nimo, the Board is covered by the same automatic content filter and report button that protect every message sent inside the app — the same protections that apply to any chat message apply to any opener posted there.

If you've tried an anonymous chat app before and gave up because "there was never anyone online," it might be worth giving a second chance to one that actually thought about that specific problem.
