# The private Feed: the space that exists only between you and your friends

There's a common misconception about anonymous chat apps: the idea that "anonymous" means everything is public and open for anyone to see. That's not quite how Nimo works — and the Feed is exactly where that difference becomes clear.

## Anonymous with strangers, closed with friends

Random matching and the Board are the public parts of the app: that's where you meet new people, without knowing who's who. The Feed is the opposite. It's a private space, visible only to you and the people you've already accepted as friends inside the app. Nobody outside that circle sees what gets posted there, and — this is the important part — not even your own friends can find out who else is your friend. Each person's friend list stays invisible to everyone else, including the people already on it.

## Why this separation exists

Meeting random people and keeping in touch with the people you've chosen to keep are two different needs, and trying to solve both in the same space usually serves neither well. If the Feed were public, it would lose its whole purpose — it would just become another version of the Board. If random matching required prior approval, it would lose the spontaneity that makes this kind of app work in the first place. By keeping them separate, each part of Nimo can be good at one thing: matching and the Board for discovering new people, the Feed for keeping what already worked out.

## What "friend" means here

Becoming friends with someone on Nimo is a decision both sides make — it's not something that happens automatically just because you exchanged a message once. And like every other part of the app, the Feed is covered by the same automatic content filter and the same report button that protect matching and the Board. Private doesn't mean unmoderated.

In the end, it's this combination — real anonymity with strangers, real privacy with the people who matter — that sets a well-thought-out anonymous chat app apart from one that just throws two people onto a screen and hopes for the best.
