# 5 safety tips for using anonymous chat without the headaches

Talking to strangers carries a risk no app erases by itself — but you can cut that risk down a lot by knowing how to use the right tools. Here are five practices that apply to any anonymous chat app, and how Nimo was built around each one of them.

## 1. You should never need to give real information just to take part

If an app asks for an email, a phone number, or any data that identifies you just so you can chat, that's already a warning sign — the more data sitting in a database, the more can leak in a security incident. Nimo has no sign-up at all: no email, no password, no number stored anywhere. An account that doesn't really exist has nothing to expose.

## 2. Report right away, not later

Waiting to "see if it gets worse" rarely helps. The moment a conversation goes somewhere it shouldn't — a scam, harassment, anything outside what was agreed — report it right there, on the message itself. In Nimo, reporting blocks whoever bothered you immediately, and removes the reported content regardless of anything else that happens afterward.

## 3. Be wary of anyone pushing to move the conversation off the app too fast

A common scam pattern is insisting on switching to a different app almost as soon as the conversation starts — usually because that other app has less moderation. If someone is pushing to leave the chat before you've even gotten to know each other, that tells you more than anything they might say about themselves.

## 4. View-once photos exist for a reason

A photo that can only be viewed once and is then deleted from the server limits the damage if the conversation goes wrong — it doesn't sit archived "just in case" anywhere. Even so, the usual common sense still applies: think twice before sending any image you wouldn't show a stranger on the street.

## 5. You can disappear at any moment

If a conversation made you uncomfortable, leaving shouldn't be a hassle. Deleting your account needs to be fast — one tap, no support ticket, no waiting period — because in the end, the best protection is always being able to walk away at no cost.

None of these five things replace a person's own judgment with technology. But together, they remove a good chunk of the risk that usually keeps people away from this kind of app — and that's exactly what Nimo was designed around.
