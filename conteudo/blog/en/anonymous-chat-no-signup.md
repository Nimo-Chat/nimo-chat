# Anonymous chat with no sign-up: how does it work, and is it safe?

"Anonymous chat" is a phrase that scares people a little before it scares them the right amount. It scares because it sounds like a dead end — talking to a stranger, knowing nothing about them, with no one to answer to if something goes wrong. And it scares too little when people don't stop to think about what "no sign-up" actually means: no email, no password, no phone number stored anywhere. That's not carelessness — it's the opposite. An account that doesn't really exist has nothing to leak.

## How matching works

When you open the app, you tap to search and enter a queue. The system tries to pair you with someone available right now, giving preference to people with similar interests and, on the paid plan, to the gender you choose. When two profiles match, a chat room opens — no previous history, no visible mutual friends, no trace of who you are outside that screen.

## What "safe" means here

Safety in anonymous chat isn't the absence of risk — it's having mechanisms to reduce and resolve risk when it shows up. In Nimo that means: a report button on every message and post, able to block whoever is bothering you instantly; an automatic filter that already catches obvious scams and spam before they reach you; and an 18+ age rating, with explicit acceptance of the terms of use right on the first screen — terms that say plainly, with no fine print, that there's zero tolerance for abusive content.

## And your data?

With no email and no password, there's nothing a database leak could expose about you. View-once photos are deleted from the server after being seen — they don't sit archived "just in case." And if you ever want to disappear for good, deleting your account is a single tap, with no support process and no waiting.

No app cancels out on its own all the caution that talking to strangers requires. But it's possible to do it in a way that leaves no trace of you behind — and that's exactly the design behind Nimo Chat.
